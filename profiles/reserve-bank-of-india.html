Decision and reasoning

The Committee unanimously held the repo rate, retaining a neutral stance while waiting for greater clarity on inflation, growth, monsoon, energy and geopolitical risks.

Inflation: 4.82% headline Consumer Price Index inflation (August 2026). Objective: 4% headline Consumer Price Index midpoint; tolerance band 2–6%.

Growth: Domestic demand remained resilient; investment and credit indicators were important supports.

Labour market: Labour conditions are assessed with growth, rural demand and high-frequency activity indicators.

Balance sheet / implementation: Liquidity Adjustment Facility, Standing Deposit Facility and Marginal Standing Facility form the operating corridor around monetary conditions.

Why the Balance Sheet Matters

Expansion can reflect asset purchases or liquidity support; contraction can reflect maturities, repayments or quantitative tightening. A larger balance sheet does not by itself mean policy is currently more accommodative—the composition and operating framework matter.

How Monetary Policy Affects the Economy

Repo rate & liquidity operations→Overnight money-market rates→Bank deposit & lending rates→Credit & domestic demand→Output & employment→Consumer-price inflation

Transmission also operates through expectations, asset prices, credit conditions and—where especially important—the exchange rate.

What to monitor

Central-bank reserves, monetary base, broad money, bank credit and the central-bank balance sheet should be read together with the operating framework.

What Could Change Policy Next?

Inflation / tighter-policy pressure

Persistent inflation, stronger wage or demand pressure, adverse energy/import-price shocks, or rising inflation expectations.

Growth / easier-policy pressure

Materially weaker activity, labour-market deterioration, tighter credit conditions or a faster and sustained decline in underlying inflation.

These are decision-relevant conditions, not a prediction of the next policy action.
