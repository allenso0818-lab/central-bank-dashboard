Decision and reasoning

Singapore’s highly open economy uses the Singapore dollar nominal effective exchange rate as the main monetary-policy instrument. The slope, width and centre of the policy band are more informative than a policy interest rate.

Inflation: 2.2% Consumer Price Index – All Items inflation (July 2026). Objective: No formal numerical inflation target.

Growth: Trade, external demand and domestic services are key activity drivers.

Labour market: Wages, unit labour costs and labour-market tightness matter for domestic inflation.

Balance sheet / implementation: Monetary operations manage domestic liquidity consistently with the exchange-rate-centred framework.

Why the Balance Sheet Matters

What Could Change Policy Next?

Inflation / tighter-policy pressure

Persistent inflation, stronger wage or demand pressure, adverse energy/import-price shocks, or rising inflation expectations.

Growth / easier-policy pressure

Materially weaker activity, labour-market deterioration, tighter credit conditions or a faster and sustained decline in underlying inflation.

These are decision-relevant conditions, not a prediction of the next policy action.
