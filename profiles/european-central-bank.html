Latest Monetary Policy Decision

Official information

Decision and reasoning

The Governing Council raised all three key rates by 25 basis points as Middle East conflict and energy pressures were expected to keep inflation above target for an extended period.

Inflation: 3.2% headline Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (August 2026). Objective: 2% inflation over the medium term.

Growth: ECB staff projected 0.9% real growth for 2026.

Labour market: Labour and wage dynamics remain part of the inflation-persistence assessment.

Balance sheet / implementation: Asset Purchase Programme and Pandemic Emergency Purchase Programme portfolios continue to decline as principal payments are no longer reinvested.

Why It Matters

The decision affects financial conditions through the institution’s own operating framework. The relevant transmission mechanism is shown below rather than assuming every central bank uses the same policy-rate system.

Dashboard interpretation: Restrictive setting strengthened in response to persistent inflation pressure. This is a descriptive synthesis of the current setting, not a forecast of the next decision.

Monetary Policy Toolkit

Deposit facility rate

Key rate steering euro-area overnight money-market rates; 2.50% after the September increase.

Main refinancing operations

Regular liquidity-providing operations; rate 2.65% after the September decision.

Marginal lending facility

Overnight central-bank credit backstop; rate 2.90%.

Balance-sheet runoff

Asset Purchase Programme and Pandemic Emergency Purchase Programme portfolios are shrinking without reinvestment.

Transmission Protection Instrument

Available to counter unwarranted disorderly market dynamics that threaten monetary-policy transmission.

How Monetary Policy Affects the Economy

ECB policy rates→Euro money-market rates→Bank funding & lending rates→Credit demand→Consumption & investment→Output, wages & inflation

Transmission also operates through expectations, asset prices, credit conditions and—where especially important—the exchange rate.

Money Supply & Liquidity Conditions

What to monitor

Central-bank reserves, monetary base, broad money, bank credit and the central-bank balance sheet should be read together with the operating framework.

Methodology: The dashboard does not use “Money Supply = Money Multiplier × Bank Reserves” as a mechanical description of modern monetary policy. Reserve and broad-money relationships vary with bank behaviour, regulation, credit demand and central-bank operations.

What Could Change Policy Next?

Inflation / tighter-policy pressure

Persistent inflation, stronger wage or demand pressure, adverse energy/import-price shocks, or rising inflation expectations.

Growth / easier-policy pressure

Materially weaker activity, labour-market deterioration, tighter credit conditions or a faster and sustained decline in underlying inflation.

These are decision-relevant conditions, not a prediction of the next policy action.
